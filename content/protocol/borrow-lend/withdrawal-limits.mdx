# Withdrawal & Borrow Limits

Every withdrawal and every new borrow is checked twice: once against your own account health, and once against the spot market's liquidity buffer. The second check protects that buffer for all of the market's depositors, so it can throttle or reject an action even when your account is comfortably above its margin requirement.

<Callout type="important">
This is **not** a margin or liquidation check. It applies on top of (not instead of) the standard withdraw margin requirement. See [Withdraw and Close Account](/protocol/getting-started/withdraw-and-close-account). A perfectly healthy account can still have a withdrawal or borrow rejected because the *market* it is withdrawing from is near its liquidity limit.
</Callout>

There are two market-level limits, one on each side of the vault:

* **Rolling withdrawal and borrow limits.** Always on. They cap how far a market's deposits can drain, and how far its borrows can grow, relative to that market's own 24-hour trailing averages.
* **A daily deposit cap.** The mirror image on the deposit side, capping how far total deposits may grow above their 24-hour average in a window. It is off by default, so most markets have none.

Both are market-wide rather than per-account. Your action can be refused because of everyone else's activity in the same window, not because of anything about your own position.

For the formulas, the parameters, and the exact conditions each check applies, see [Withdrawal Limit Mechanics](/protocol/how-it-works/withdrawal-limit-mechanics).

## Where this shows up for you

* **Withdrawals and borrows**: the most common place you'll see this. See [Withdraw and Close Account](/protocol/getting-started/withdraw-and-close-account).
* **Subaccount transfers**: moving collateral between your own subaccounts still draws down the source market, so it is checked too.
* **Token swaps**: the sell leg of a swap is a withdrawal from that market's perspective. See [What is Borrow & Lend?](/protocol/borrow-lend).
* **Deposits**: checked against the daily deposit cap, when the market has one configured.

Opening a borrow is checked more strictly than reducing a deposit. A withdrawal that takes your balance in a market to zero and then keeps going has to clear the market's borrow ceiling as well as its deposit floor.

## If your action is refused

The limits reset as the market's 24-hour averages roll forward, so the same action often succeeds later without anything else changing. In the meantime:

* **Withdraw less.** The check is against the market's resulting balance, so a smaller amount may fit under the limit.
* **Withdraw a different asset.** Each spot market has its own limits, so a market under pressure does not affect your other collateral.
* **Repay a borrow first.** Repayments are never blocked by these limits, and reducing the market's borrows loosens the check for everyone.

If your whole position in that market is very small, and you have only ever been a net depositor, you can usually still withdraw your principal even while the market is at its limit. There is a narrow exception for exactly this case, though the total it releases per market is capped.

<Callout type="warning">
If a withdrawal or borrow fails with a "daily withdraw limit" style error, or a deposit fails with a daily deposit limit error, check whether the market itself is near its rolling limit before assuming there is a problem with your account. These are market-wide conditions, not account-specific ones. A separate "market withdraws paused" error means an admin has paused the market; see [Block Conditions](/protocol/risk-and-safety/block-conditions).
</Callout>
